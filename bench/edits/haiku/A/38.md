# Product design principles

The Design Systems team at Fathom Bank maintains these principles. They are linked from every design review template.

These principles were agreed in a workshop with 26 designers and engineers on 2026-09-17. They apply to all customer-facing apps.

## Clarity over cleverness

Label every control with the action it performs. A customer should never have to guess what a button will do, especially when money is moving. Clever wording and unlabeled icons make people slow down and second-guess themselves.

- **Do:** label buttons with the action, such as "Send transfer".
- **Avoid:** icons without text for money movement.

## Show the money

Fees, limits and totals belong before the customer commits, not after. When the cost of an action appears only on the final screen, customers feel tricked, and they are right to.

- **Do:** show fees before confirmation.
- **Avoid:** revealing fees on the final screen.

## Forgive mistakes

Customers make mistakes, and the product should make them easy to recover from. Where a change can be reversed safely, give the customer a short window to undo it.

- **Do:** allow undo for 10 seconds after a transfer is scheduled.
- **Avoid:** making cancellation a phone call.

## Accessible by default

Accessibility is a baseline, not an add-on. Text must have enough contrast to read comfortably, and no status should depend on color alone.

- **Do:** meet a contrast ratio of 4.5 to 1.
- **Avoid:** relying on color alone for status.

## Calm, not urgent

Customers make better decisions when they are not rushed. State deadlines plainly, and let people take the time they need.

- **Do:** state plain deadlines.
- **Avoid:** countdown timers that push decisions.

## Explain the why

When we hold or delay a customer's action, say why. A customer who is left guessing assumes the worst, and a clear reason lets them act or wait with confidence.

- **Do:** tell customers why a payment is on hold, and what they can do next.
- **Avoid:** showing "Payment on hold" with no reason or next step.

> If a customer has to read it twice, we wrote it wrong.
>
> Ines Albrecht, Head of Design

> **Note: disagreements go to the design council.**
> If you disagree with a principle, raise it at the monthly design council, not in individual design reviews.
