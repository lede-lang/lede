# Write It Down Before You Build It

*By Daniel Reyes, Principal Engineer at Thornbury Labs*

Most of the expensive mistakes I have seen on software projects were not mistakes of code. They were mistakes of assumption, and they were made long before anyone opened an editor. The fix is cheap and almost embarrassingly simple. Write the thing down before you build it.

## The meeting that should have been a page

A familiar pattern goes like this. Someone has an idea. A meeting gets booked for an hour, with six people in it. Half of them have not read anything, so the first twenty minutes go to explaining the problem. Someone raises a concern that was already settled last quarter. The meeting ends with a vague sense that everyone agrees, and a follow-up to write down what was decided. Nobody writes it down, and the team starts building on a shared memory that is slightly different for each person.

A page would have done better. A page forces the author to say the problem out loud, to put the options side by side, and to commit to a recommendation. It also gives the reader time, so they can think before they respond.

## What a design note is

A design note is one to two pages. The author writes it before any code exists, and the people who need to react read it on their own time. It describes the problem, the options that were considered, the choice that was made, and the reasons for that choice. The discussion happens in the comments, and the note is the record of where the discussion ended up.

Notes are read asynchronously. That matters more than it sounds. A reader can spend ten minutes on a page and come back with questions that are specific and useful. A meeting rarely produces that.

## What it is not

A design note is not a specification. A specification tries to describe every behavior of a system in advance, and it goes stale the moment the first assumption breaks. A note is shorter and makes fewer promises. It records what we think is true and why we chose a path.

A design note is also not a permission slip. Writing one does not hand the decision to a committee. The author still decides, and the note exists so that the decision is informed and visible.

## The cost

At Thornbury, the median design note took about three hours to write. That is not nothing, and I have heard the objection many times. Three hours is a day of work for a team that is already short on time.

Then I look at the other side of the ledger. In 2026, we counted eleven projects where a design note caught a wrong assumption before any code was written. Each of those would otherwise have turned into weeks of rework, and some would have shipped the wrong thing entirely. Three hours against weeks is not a close call.

> "The page is where you find out what you do not know."

## Start small

You do not need a template, a review board or a new tool to begin. Pick the next piece of work that will take more than a week, and write a page about it before the first ticket is opened. Share it with two people who will be affected by it, and ask them to comment within two days.

Every note should answer four questions:

- What problem are we solving?
- Why now?
- What did we consider?
- How will we know it worked?

If you can answer those four questions on one page, you are ready to build. If you cannot, you have just found out what you do not know, and that is the cheapest time to learn it.
