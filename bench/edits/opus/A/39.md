# How We Cut Our Checkout Page Weight by Half

*Sam Whitlock, Kiln Coffee Roasters Engineering*

## Where we started

Earlier this year we sat down with a mid-range phone, a throttled connection, and our own checkout page. It was not a fun afternoon. The page weighed **1.9 MB** and took **4.8 seconds** to become interactive. That's almost five seconds of someone holding a bag of coffee in their cart, tapping a button that isn't listening yet.

Nobody had made one big mistake. The page had just grown, one reasonable addition at a time, the way pages do. So we opened the network panel, sorted by size, and started at the top.

After a few weeks of work, the checkout page weighs **0.95 MB** and becomes interactive in **2.1 seconds** on the same phone. Here's how we got there.

## The three big wins

Most of the savings came from three changes:

1. **We replaced a 310 KB date picker with the native one.** We used it to let people schedule a subscription start date. That's a lot of JavaScript for picking a day. The browser's built-in date input does the job, works well with screen readers, and costs us nothing to ship.
2. **We lazy-loaded the reviews widget.** Reviews are lovely, but nobody needs them before they can type an address. The widget now loads only when someone scrolls down to it, so it no longer competes with the parts of the page people actually use to check out.
3. **We served product photos at the size they are displayed.** We were sending full-resolution bag shots and letting the browser shrink them into small thumbnails. Generating images at the displayed sizes cut a big chunk of bytes, and the photos look exactly the same.

None of this is clever. That's sort of the point: the biggest wins were things we'd been meaning to get to for ages.

## What did not work

We also tried inlining all our styles into the page, on the theory that skipping a stylesheet request would make the first load faster. It did help the first visit a little. But it made repeat visits slower, because the browser could no longer cache the stylesheet, so every page view re-downloaded all of the CSS. A lot of our customers are subscribers who come back often, so that was the wrong trade. We rolled it back and kept the stylesheet external.

## What is next

We launched the lighter checkout on **2026-08-04**. In the four weeks that followed, mobile conversion rose from **2.6%** to **3.1%**. We'd love to say the speedup did all of that, but we can't prove it. Seasonality, promotions and plain noise all play a part, and we didn't run this as a controlled experiment. What we can say is that the page is faster, and nothing got worse.

Next, we want to apply the same treatment to the product pages, and we're setting up a page-weight budget in our build so the checkout doesn't quietly creep back up to 1.9 MB.
