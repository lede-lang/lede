# Product Design Principles

**Fathom Bank · Design Systems team**

These principles were agreed in a workshop with 26 designers and engineers on 2026-09-17. They apply to all customer-facing apps, and every design review should be checked against them.

## 1. Clarity over cleverness

Customers come to us to move and manage money, not to decode an interface. When a clever pattern and a plain one both work, choose the plain one. Every control should say what it does, especially when it moves money.

- **Do:** Label buttons with the action they perform.
- **Avoid:** Icons without text for money movement.

## 2. Show the money

Customers should never be surprised by what something costs. Fees, rates and totals belong where the decision is made, not after it. A customer who finds a fee late feels tricked, even if the fee was fair.

- **Do:** Show fees before confirmation.
- **Avoid:** Revealing fees on the final screen.

## 3. Forgive mistakes

People mistype, tap the wrong account and change their minds. The product should make recovering from that easy and quick, so that a slip doesn't turn into a support call or a lost payment.

- **Do:** Allow undo for 10 seconds after a transfer is scheduled.
- **Avoid:** Making cancellation a phone call.

## 4. Accessible by default

Accessibility is a baseline we build from the start, not a feature we add at the end. Every customer should be able to read, navigate and act on every screen, whatever their vision, motor ability or device.

- **Do:** Meet a contrast ratio of 4.5 to 1.
- **Avoid:** Relying on color alone for status.

## 5. Calm, not urgent

Money decisions deserve a clear head. We give customers the facts and the time to decide, and we don't invent pressure to speed them up.

- **Do:** State plain deadlines.
- **Avoid:** Countdown timers that push decisions.

## 6. Explain the why

When we stop or delay something a customer asked for, we tell them why. A reason turns a frustrating dead end into something they can understand and act on, and it saves a call to support.

- **Do:** Tell customers why a payment is on hold and what they can do next.
- **Avoid:** Showing "Payment on hold" with no reason.
