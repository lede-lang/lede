Fathom Bank · Design Systems

Product design principles

These principles were agreed in a workshop with 26 designers and engineers on 2026-09-17. They apply to all customer-facing apps, and every design review should check work against them.

01Clarity over cleverness

Customers come to us to get something done with their money, not to admire the interface. When a clever pattern and a plain one compete, the plain one wins. Every control should say what it does in words a customer would use.

  • DoLabel buttons with the action.
  • AvoidIcons without text for money movement.

02Show the money

Customers should never be surprised by what something costs. Fees, rates and totals belong in plain view at the point where a customer is deciding, not after they have committed.

  • DoShow fees before confirmation.
  • AvoidRevealing fees on the final screen.

03Forgive mistakes

People tap the wrong thing, enter the wrong amount and change their minds. Our job is to make recovering from that quick and easy, inside the app, without making the customer feel at fault.

  • DoAllow undo for 10 seconds after a transfer is scheduled.
  • AvoidMaking cancellation a phone call.

04Accessible by default

Accessibility is part of the design from the start, not a fix applied at the end. Every customer should be able to read, understand and operate every screen, whatever their vision, device or abilities.

  • DoMeet a contrast ratio of 4.5 to 1.
  • AvoidRelying on color alone for status.

05Calm, not urgent

Financial decisions deserve a clear head. We give customers the facts they need, including real deadlines, and then let them decide at their own pace. We never manufacture pressure.

  • DoState plain deadlines.
  • AvoidCountdown timers that push decisions.

06Explain the why

When we stop, delay or decline something, customers deserve to know the reason. A plain explanation, and what happens next, turns a frustrating moment into one the customer can act on.

  • DoTell customers why a payment is on hold and when it will clear.
  • AvoidShowing a bare "On hold" status with no reason.

"If a customer has to read it twice, we wrote it wrong."

Ines Albrecht, Head of Design
Note: Disagreements with a principle go to the monthly design council, not to individual reviews.
