Who each plan suits

  • Basic

    $168 a year

    The lowest premium, with the lowest annual maximum ($1,000) and the highest deductible ($75). It suits people who expect little more than routine care and no orthodontia.

  • Plus

    $336 a year

    A higher annual maximum ($1,500) and a lower deductible ($50) than Basic, still without orthodontia. It suits people who want more cover for their own care but have no braces to plan for.

  • Premier

    $492 a year

    The first plan with orthodontia: 50% up to a $1,500 lifetime maximum, with a $2,500 annual maximum and a $25 deductible. It suits employees who want orthodontia cover, including those whose children will need braces.

  • Family Max

    $756 a year

    No deductible, a $3,000 annual maximum and orthodontia up to a $2,000 lifetime maximum. It suits families with ongoing orthodontia. Over Premier it adds $500 of annual maximum and $500 of lifetime orthodontia, and removes the $25 deductible.

38

employees said in the pulse survey that they have children who will need braces soon. That is why orthodontia matters in this choice.

Recommendation

Make Premier the default plan

  • It is the cheapest plan with orthodontia, which the 38 employees expecting braces for their children will need.
  • It costs $156 a year more than Plus and adds orthodontia, a higher annual maximum and a lower deductible.

Why not Family Max? It costs $264 a year more than Premier and only pays off for families with ongoing orthodontia. Those employees can still choose it before open enrollment closes on 2026-11-20.
