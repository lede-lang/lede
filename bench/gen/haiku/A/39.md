# How We Cut Our Checkout Page Weight in Half

*By Sam Whitlock*

Our checkout page had grown heavy over the years, the way pages do. Every feature we added brought a little more code and a few more images with it. This is the story of how we pulled it back down, and what we learned on the way.

## Where we started

The checkout page weighed 1.9 MB. On a mid-range phone, it took 4.8 seconds to become interactive. That is a long time for someone who is ready to pay, and we could see it in our funnel. Most of the weight came from a handful of large pieces that nobody had looked at in a while.

## The three big wins

1. We replaced a 310 KB date picker with the native one. The native control is already on the phone, so it costs us nothing to ship.
2. We lazy-loaded the reviews widget. It now loads only when a customer scrolls near it, so it no longer competes with the payment form.
3. We served product photos at the size they are displayed. A thumbnail no longer arrives as a full-resolution image.

After all three, the page weighed 0.9 MB, and it became interactive in 2.1 seconds.

## What did not work

We tried inlining all of our styles into the page, to avoid a separate request. It backfired. Repeat visits got slower, because the browser could no longer reuse a cached stylesheet across pages. We reverted that change within a week.

## What is next

The mobile conversion rate rose from 2.6 percent to 3.1 percent in the four weeks after we launched on 2026-08-04. We are careful not to claim that the speedup caused all of that gain. Other changes shipped in the same window, and we cannot separate them out cleanly.
