Budget meeting, 10 November 2026

Replacing 18 delivery vans: diesel, hybrid or electric

Prepared for Tomasz Wieczorek, operations director. We replace the fleet over the next two years. This page compares five-year cost per van and sets out the risks of each option.

Options

Diesel

  • Purchase: $41,000
  • Fuel: $9,800 a year
  • Maintenance: $3,400 a year
  • Low-emission zone fee from 1 January 2028: $12.50 a day

Hybrid

  • Purchase: $46,500
  • Fuel: $6,900 a year
  • Maintenance: $2,900 a year
  • No range concerns; still has tailpipe emissions

Electric

  • Purchase: $58,200 after the $7,500 incentive
  • Charger installation: $4,200
  • Electricity: $3,100 a year
  • Maintenance: $1,700 a year
  • Real-world range: about 180 miles in summer, 150 in winter

Risks

  • Electric range in winter. The electric van's real-world range is about 150 miles in winter and 180 in summer. Our longest route is 140 miles a day, so the two longest routes are tight in January. That is why the plan gives the electric vans the 12 shorter routes.
  • Charging. Six depot chargers have to serve 12 electric vans overnight. The charging schedule needs checking before the first vans arrive.
  • Hybrid emissions. Hybrids have no range worries, but they still produce tailpipe emissions. Our five-year figures do not price that in.
  • Diesel fee. From 1 January 2028, diesel vans are subject to a low-emission zone fee of $12.50 a day. That fee is not included in the diesel totals above.
  • Incentive. The electric price assumes the $7,500 incentive applies.

Recommendation

Buy electric for the 12 shorter routes, and hybrid for the 6 longest.

This mixed fleet comes to $1,609,800 for the 18 vans: 12 at $86,400 and 6 at $95,500. That is about $316,200 less than staying with diesel at $1,926,000, and about $109,200 less than an all-hybrid fleet at $1,719,000. It also avoids the diesel low-emission zone fee. The hybrids cover the longest routes because of the winter range limit.
