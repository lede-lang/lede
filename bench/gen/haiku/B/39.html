How We Cut Our Checkout Page Weight in Half

Sam Whitlock, Kiln Coffee Roasters engineering blog

Where we started

Our checkout page had grown the way most checkout pages do. Every feature added a script, a stylesheet or an image, and nobody took any of them away. By the time we measured it, the page weighed 1.9 MB. On a mid-range phone it took 4.8 seconds to become interactive. That is a long time to stare at a spinner when you are about to pay for coffee.

The three big wins

We did not find one magic fix. We found three ordinary ones, and each was a small change.

  1. We replaced a 310 KB date picker with the native one that the browser already ships.
  2. We lazy-loaded the reviews widget, so it only loads when someone scrolls near it.
  3. We served product photos at the size they are displayed, instead of the full-size originals.

Together these brought the page down to 0.9 MB, and it became interactive in 2.1 seconds on the same phone.

What did not work

We tried inlining all of our styles into the page to avoid a second request. It looked sensible on paper. In practice, repeat visits got slower, because the browser could no longer cache the stylesheet and reuse it across pages. We reverted that change within a week.

What is next

Mobile conversion rose from 2.6 percent to 3.1 percent in the four weeks after we launched on 2026-08-04. We are pleased with that, but we cannot prove the speedup caused all of it. Pricing changes, a new promotion and seasonal traffic all happened in the same window. What we can say is that the faster page did not hurt.
