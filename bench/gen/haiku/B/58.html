Time to first release

Estimated months from start to first release, for each approach.

The first release must ship by 15 January 2027 at the latest. Flutter's 7-month estimate misses that date unless we cut scope. The native estimate of 11 months misses it by more.

Pros and cons

Fully native

For: full offline and push support, smooth barcode scanning, app store presence.

Against: the longest route to release at 11 months, and the highest cost at $1.45 million. Two separate apps for a team of 4.

Flutter

For: one codebase for both platforms. Full offline and push support, app store presence, and good barcode scanning. Seven months and $0.92 million.

Against: 7 months still misses the January date unless we cut scope. Barcode scanning is good, not as smooth as native.

Progressive web app

For: the fastest, at 4 months, and the cheapest, at $0.48 million.

Against: offline support is only partial, push is limited on iOS, barcode scanning is unreliable, and there is no app store presence.

Recommendation

We lean toward Flutter. It is the middle option on time and cost, and it keeps the offline, push and app store features our customers expect.

To be plain about it: 7 months misses the 15 January 2027 date unless we cut scope. The two cuts we would make are to drop in-app chat support, and to postpone the recipe feature. We have not yet estimated how much time each cut saves, and that number is what the council needs before choosing.
