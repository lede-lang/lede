Product design principles

These principles were agreed in a workshop with 26 designers and engineers on 2026-09-17. They apply to all customer-facing apps. Link to this page from every design review template.

Clarity over cleverness

A customer should understand what a control does before they touch it. Clever labels, unfamiliar icons and novel patterns cost attention, and attention is scarce when money is involved. Choose the plain option, and test it with someone who has never seen the screen.

  • Do label buttons with the action, such as “Send payment”.
  • Avoid icons without text for money movement.

Show the money

Customers should see every fee and cost before they confirm an action. A fee that appears only on the last screen feels like a trap, even when it is disclosed. Put the full cost where the decision is made.

  • Do show fees before confirmation.
  • Avoid revealing fees on the final screen.

Forgive mistakes

People make mistakes, especially under time pressure. Every irreversible-looking action should have a way back, and the way back should be simple enough to use without help.

  • Do allow undo for 10 seconds after a transfer is scheduled.
  • Avoid making cancellation a phone call.

Accessible by default

Accessibility is part of the first design, not a later fix. Contrast, labels and non-colour cues help every customer, not only those who use assistive technology.

  • Do meet a contrast ratio of 4.5 to 1.
  • Avoid relying on colour alone for status.

Calm, not urgent

Banking decisions are rarely better when rushed. We state deadlines plainly and let customers decide at their own pace. Pressure tactics erode trust, and they do not belong in our products.

  • Do state plain deadlines.
  • Avoid countdown timers that push decisions.
“If a customer has to read it twice, we wrote it wrong.”
— Ines Albrecht, Head of Design
Disagreeing with a principle Disagreements with a principle go to the monthly design council, not to individual design reviews.
