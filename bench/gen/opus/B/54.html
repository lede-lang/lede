Summary

On 2026-09-21 a weekly workout reminder campaign was meant to go out once to 1,240,000 users. A retry bug in the queue consumer re-sent each message six extra times, so every recipient got the same notification seven times: 7,440,000 duplicates over 38 minutes.

The on-call engineer paused the campaign sender and purged the queue, which stopped the duplicates at 16:38 Pacific. Push opt-outs reached 3.4% of recipients against a normal 0.2%, and the app store rating fell from 4.6 to 4.3 over the following week.

Contributing factors

The push consumer treated a slow downstream acknowledgement as a failure and retried the send. Because messages carried no idempotency key, nothing downstream could recognise a retry as a message it had already delivered, so each retry reached the user as a fresh notification.

The campaign tool allowed a send to the entire user base in one step, without a staged ramp. A small first stage would have exposed the duplicates to a fraction of users before the rest of the audience was reached.

Once the problem was known, stopping it was slowed by the pause switch for the sender being undocumented. It took 7 minutes to find.

These are gaps in the system and its safeguards. The change that introduced the retry behaviour went through our normal review; no individual is at fault, and the fixes below address the conditions that let one bug reach every user seven times.

Customer impact

Every one of the 1,240,000 users in the campaign received the same workout reminder seven times within about half an hour, 7,440,000 unwanted notifications in total. For many people that was an annoyance; for 42,160 of them it was enough to turn off push notifications from us altogether, cutting off a channel they had chosen to keep. The frustration also showed in the app store, where our rating slipped from 4.6 to 4.3 over the following week.
