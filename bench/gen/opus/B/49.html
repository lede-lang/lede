How Parcelwise delivers shipment webhooks

Version dated 2026-10-05

When an order ships, Parcelwise sends a signed POST to your webhook endpoint. If your endpoint fails or is too slow, we retry on a fixed schedule; after the sixth failed attempt the event is parked for replay.

Delivery sequence

!

Respond within 10 seconds. A response slower than 10 seconds counts as a failure, even if it eventually succeeds, and the event is retried.

  1. Order Service publishes order.shipped to the Event Bus.
  2. The Event Bus hands the event to a Delivery Worker.
  3. The worker sends a signed POST /webhooks to your endpoint and waits up to 10 seconds.
  4. Your endpoint returns 503 Service Unavailable, so the attempt fails.
  5. The worker requeues the event with a 1 minute delay.
  6. The Event Bus delivers it again as attempt 2.
  7. The worker sends POST /webhooks again.
  8. Your endpoint returns 200 OK.
  9. The worker acknowledges the event and delivery is complete.
  10. If every attempt fails, the worker parks the event in the Dead Letter Queue after attempt 6.

If attempt 6 fails, the event is parked in the Dead Letter Queue. Elapsed times are approximate and exclude the time each attempt takes, up to 10 seconds.

Good to know

  • !

    Slow responses are failures

    Any response slower than 10 seconds counts as a failed attempt. Acknowledge quickly and do heavy processing after you respond.

  • 1

    Verify the signature

    Every request carries an X-Parcelwise-Signature header: an HMAC-SHA256 of the request body.

  • 2

    Events can arrive out of order

    Compare the sequence number before applying an event, so an older update never overwrites a newer one.

  • 3

    Parked events can be replayed

    Events in the Dead Letter Queue are kept for 14 days and can be replayed from the dashboard.
