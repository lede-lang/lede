Replacing our 18 delivery vans

Over the next two years we replace the whole delivery fleet. This page compares diesel, hybrid and electric on five-year cost and on how well each fits our routes, and recommends a mixed fleet: electric for the 12 shorter routes, hybrid for the 6 longest.

  • 18 vans
  • Longest route: 140 miles a day
  • 6 overnight chargers possible at the depot
  • Prepared by Tomasz Wieczorek, Operations Director

Risks

  • Winter range is tight for electric on the longest routes.The longest route is 140 miles a day. Electric range drops to about 150 miles in winter, so the two longest routes would be tight in January, with little margin for detours or delays.
  • Charging capacity at the depot.We can charge overnight with 6 chargers. With 12 electric vans, that is two vans per charger, so overnight charging has to be scheduled and confirmed before we commit.
  • Hybrids still emit.They remove range worries but produce tailpipe emissions, so they are a partial step, not a full one.
  • Diesel gets more expensive from 2028.The city low-emission zone fee of $12.50 a day per diesel van starts January 1, 2028, which is inside the life of any van we buy now.
  • The electric cost depends on the incentive.The $58,200 purchase price assumes the $7,500 incentive.

Recommendation

Buy electric for the 12 shorter routes and hybrid for the 6 longest.

Electric is cheapest over five years. Putting hybrids on the six longest routes takes the winter range risk off the table where it matters most.
