Pros and cons

Harbor Point

Pros

  • Lab-ready now: no fit-out bill and no construction to manage.
  • Shortest commute, 24 minutes at the median.
  • Move-in on Feb 1 leaves two months of slack before our lease ends.

Cons

  • Most expensive: $1,392,000 a year, $288,000 more than Mill District.
  • Locks in the highest rate for 10 years.

Mill District Leaning

Pros

  • $288,000 a year less base rent than Harbor Point.
  • Offices and lab under one roof, so the team stays together.
  • Commute is 31 minutes at the median, 11 minutes shorter than Northgate Park.

Cons

  • We pay about $1.1M to finish the lab, and fit-outs can run over in cost and time.
  • A 7-year term means we face this decision again sooner, and we recover the fit-out over 7 years rather than 10.
  • Move-in on Mar 1 leaves only one month before our lease ends, so a delay would hurt.

Northgate Park

Pros

  • Lowest base rent: $912,000 a year.
  • Earliest move-in, Jan 15, 2027.

Cons

  • No lab space: we would need a second lease nearby, at a cost not included here.
  • Splits the team across two sites.
  • Longest commute, 42 minutes at the median, 18 minutes more than Harbor Point.

Recommendation

We lean toward Mill District.

Harbor Point is the easiest move, but it costs $288,000 a year more in base rent than Mill District, and it commits us to that rate for 10 years. Northgate Park looks cheapest on paper, but it has no lab, so it splits the team across two sites and adds a second lease we have not priced. Mill District keeps everyone in one building at a rent well below Harbor Point.

What we are accepting with Mill District

  • The fit-out is a real cost. About $1.1M is ours to pay, and it narrows the gap with Harbor Point to roughly $131,000 a year once spread over the term.
  • The 7-year term is a real downside. We would be looking for space again around 2034, with less time to recover what we put into the lab.
  • The timeline is tight. Move-in on March 1, 2027 leaves one month before our current lease ends on March 31.
