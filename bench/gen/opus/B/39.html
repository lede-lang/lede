How We Cut Our Checkout Page Weight in Half

By Sam Whitlock

Checkout is the last page between someone and a bag of fresh coffee, so it should be the fastest page we have. For a long time it was the opposite. This is the story of how we slimmed it down, what worked, and the one idea we had to throw away.

Where we started

When we finally measured it properly, the numbers were a little embarrassing. Our checkout page weighed 1.9 MB, and on a mid-range phone it took 4.8 seconds to become interactive. That is almost five seconds of someone staring at a payment form they can't use yet.

None of it came from one bad decision. It was years of reasonable additions, each one small on its own, piling up into something heavy. So rather than look for a single culprit, we went through the page piece by piece and asked what each part was costing us.

Page weight
1.9 MB→0.9 MB
Time to interactive
4.8 s→2.1 s

The three big wins

Most of the savings came from three changes:

  1. We replaced a 310 KB date picker with the native one. We only used it to choose a delivery date, and the browser's built-in picker does that job well on every device we care about.
  2. We lazy-loaded the reviews widget. Reviews are nice to have on checkout, but nobody needs them before the form works. Now they load after the parts people actually came for.
  3. We served product photos at the size they are displayed. We had been sending large images and letting the browser shrink them into small thumbnails. Sizing them properly on the server cut a lot of bytes for no visible difference.

Together with some smaller cleanup, those changes brought the page down to 0.9 MB, and it now becomes interactive in 2.1 seconds on the same mid-range phone.

What did not work

Not every idea survived. We tried inlining all of our styles into the page, on the theory that skipping a separate stylesheet request would speed up the first render. It did help a first visit a little, but it made repeat visits slower. Inlined styles can't be cached on their own, so returning customers downloaded the same CSS every single time. Since a lot of our customers are regulars reordering their favorite beans, that was the wrong trade, and we rolled it back.

What is next

We launched the lighter checkout on 2026-08-04. In the four weeks after launch, mobile conversion rose from 2.6 percent to 3.1 percent. We'd love to say the speedup did all of that, but we can't prove it. Other things change in four weeks too, from seasonal demand to marketing campaigns, so we're treating the result as encouraging rather than conclusive.

What we are confident about is that a faster checkout is a better checkout. Next, we want to apply the same piece-by-piece review to the rest of the shop, and to keep an eye on page weight so it doesn't creep back up.
