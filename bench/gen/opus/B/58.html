How we rebuild the delivery app

We are relaunching the delivery app and need to choose how to build it: fully native, Flutter, or a progressive web app. We lean toward Flutter, with two scope cuts, because none of the full-scope options fits our deadline as well.

  • 4 mobile developers
  • First release by January 15, 2027 at the latest
  • Owner: Wanjiru Kamau, Engineering Manager

Plainly: at full scope, Flutter's 7 months misses the January 15, 2027 date. It only works if we cut scope (see the recommendation). Native misses it by an even wider margin.

Pros and cons

Native

Pros

  • Best experience: smooth barcode scanning, full offline and push.
  • In both app stores.

Cons

  • Slowest: 11 months to first release.
  • Most expensive: $1.45M.
  • Two separate apps to build and maintain with 4 developers.

Flutter

Pros

  • One codebase for iOS and Android.
  • Full offline basket, saved lists and push notifications.
  • In both app stores; barcode scanning is good.
  • $0.53M cheaper than native.

Cons

  • 7 months misses January 15 unless we cut scope.
  • Scanning is good, not quite as smooth as native.

Web app (PWA)

Pros

  • Fastest: 4 months to first release.
  • Cheapest: $0.48M.

Cons

  • Not in the app stores.
  • Barcode scanning is unreliable.
  • Offline basket only partly works; push is limited on iOS.

Recommendation

Build with Flutter, and cut scope to make the January date.

Flutter gives us the app-store presence, offline basket, push notifications and scanning that a grocery app needs, at $0.92M, from a single codebase our 4 developers can carry. The web app is faster and cheaper, but falls short on exactly those features. Native costs more and takes longer.

To be clear: at full scope Flutter misses January 15, 2027. To get a first release out in time, we would make two scope cuts:

  • 1. Drop in-app chat supportNot part of the first release.
  • 2. Postpone the recipe featureMoved to a later release.
