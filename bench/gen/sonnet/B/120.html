How to read your electricity bill

A plain-language guide for residential customers. Rates on this page are effective 2026-11-01. A kilowatt-hour (kWh) is the unit we use to measure the power you use.

What each charge means

Service charge: $14.50 a month
A fixed monthly charge for being connected to the grid. It does not change with how much power you use.
Delivery charge: $0.045 per kWh
The cost of bringing power to your home. You pay this for every kWh, at any time of day.
Supply charge: depends on the time
The cost of the power itself. The price changes through the day. The next chart shows the three prices.
Sales tax: 5.2 percent
Applied to the total of your charges.
Paying your bill
Your bill is due 21 days after the bill date. After that, we add a late payment fee of 1.5 percent to unpaid balances.

Ways to lower your bill

  1. Run the dishwasher and laundry after 21:00. You move that use out of peak hours.
  2. Precool the house before 16:00, then set the thermostat 2 degrees higher during peak.
  3. Charge electric vehicles after midnight. From 00:00 to 06:00 you pay the lowest supply price, $0.08.

A quick example. Moving 10 kWh from peak to off-peak saves $1.90 in supply charges, before tax. Moving it to super off-peak saves $2.30.
